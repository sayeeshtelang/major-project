================================================================================
          FUTURE PEAK LOAD PREDICTION MODEL & FORMULA EXPLANATION
================================================================================

1. OVERVIEW & CONCEPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In electrical power engineering, total daily energy demand (measured in 
Million Units or MU, where 1 MU = 1 GWh = 1,000 MWh) represents cumulative energy 
consumed over a 24-hour period. However, total daily energy alone does NOT tell 
grid operators the instantaneous peak capacity required to prevent blackouts, 
grid frequency drops, and transformer overloads.

Peak Load Prediction calculates the maximum instantaneous power demand (in Megawatts, MW) 
expected during peak operational hours. It accounts for load distribution, peak hour 
coincidence (simultaneous peak usage by industrial, commercial, and residential sectors), 
and grid contingency safety margins.


2. MATHEMATICAL FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Future Peak Load (P_peak) in Megawatts (MW) is derived using the following 
physically grounded power system formula:

    P_peak (MW) = [ ( E_24h * 1000 ) / ( 24 * LF ) ] * K_coincidence * ( 1 + alpha_contingency )

Where:
  • E_24h             = Predicted 24-Hour Total Energy Demand (in MU / GWh)
  • 1000              = Conversion factor from MU (GWh) to MWh
  • 24                = Hours in a day
  • LF                = Grid Daily Load Factor (nominal value: 0.78 or 78%)
                        [LF = Average Demand / Peak Demand]
  • K_coincidence     = Peak Coincidence Multiplier (nominal value: 1.12)
                        Accounts for sharp simultaneous demand surges during evening peak.
  • alpha_contingency = Weather & Spinning Reserve Contingency Buffer (nominal value: 0.05 or 5%)


3. STEP-BY-STEP DERIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Calculate Average Power Demand (P_avg) in MW
        P_avg (MW) = ( E_24h * 1000 MWh ) / 24 hours
        For E_24h = 100 MU:
        P_avg = (100 * 1000) / 24 = 4,166.67 MW

Step 2: Base Peak Power from Load Factor (LF = 0.78)
        P_base_peak = P_avg / LF
        P_base_peak = 4,166.67 / 0.78 = 5,341.88 MW

Step 3: Apply Coincidence Multiplier (K_coincidence = 1.12)
        P_coincident = 5,341.88 * 1.12 = 5,982.91 MW

Step 4: Apply Contingency Reserve Buffer (alpha = 5%)
        P_peak = 5,982.91 * 1.05 = 6,282.05 MW


4. KEY METRICS PRODUCED FOR GRID MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Predicted Peak Demand (MW): The maximum instantaneous power capacity expected.
2. Average Power Demand (MW): Baseline continuous demand throughout 24 hours.
3. Peak Surge Multiplier: Ratio of Peak Load to Average Load (approx 1.507x or +50.7% surge).
4. Estimated Peak Window: 18:00 - 22:00 (Evening Peak Hour Window).
5. Recommended Spinning Reserve: Minimum required generation headroom to maintain grid balance.


5. APPLICATION IN DASHBOARD & OPTIMIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
By presenting the Future Peak Load in MW on the executive dashboard, dispatchers and 
energy planners can immediately assess whether generation assets (coal, gas, hydro, renewables) 
and storage reserves can comfortably handle the peak instantaneous load without risking 
load shedding or localized voltage sags.
